# Art-direction families

These are starting logics, not fixed themes. Give every proposal an article-specific name.

## Product demonstration

Promise → friction → real interaction proof → capability → try it. Use screenshots, annotations, a workflow, and an installation console. Concrete and scannable; less intimate.

## Maker's field notes

Moment of frustration → experiment → making process → result → reflection. Use quiet typography, notebook notes, milestones, sketches, and sparse screenshots. Strong author presence; slower feature scanning.

## Visual explainer

Question → mental model → mechanism → example → takeaway. Lead with a visual thesis and use diagrams, comparisons, and annotated examples. Exceptionally clear; can feel instructional.

## Editorial column

Provocative claim → evidence → counterpoint → synthesis. Use typographic headlines, continuous reading, rules, pull quotes, and source notes—not rounded cards. Elegant; poor for long procedures.

## Evidence dossier

Executive finding → evidence groups → comparison → implication. Use metrics, charts, tables, timelines, captions, and confidence notes. Credible; requires reliable evidence.

## Cultural magazine

Atmosphere → scene → idea → resonance. Use an image-led opener, cinematic pacing, chapter spreads, photography or editorial illustration. Memorable; less efficient for dense facts.

## Scenic portal

One framing metaphor → immersive scene → narrow centered promise → quiet action or transition. The visual frame must belong to the article's story—a window, doorway, screen, telescope, map fold, book spread, or other portal—rather than serving as decoration. Use one full-width precomposed scene for the opening or a major chapter break, with very few words and strong atmosphere. Read [scenic-immersion.md](scenic-immersion.md). Memorable and emotionally immediate; unsuitable for evidence-dense or step-heavy sections.

## Newsroom signal brief

What happened → what matters → noise → what to watch. Use a timestamped lead, signal/noise split, timeline, source captures, and watchlist. Immediate; dates quickly.

## Diversity test

Three proposals must differ on at least four axes: opening composition, information order, component grammar, visual source/type, typography, and density. If two can be converted by changing colors and radii, replace one.
